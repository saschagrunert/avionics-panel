edges within 0.25 mm. The CPL follows JLC's convention for the bottom side as KiBot's JLCPCB filter and the
kicad-jlcpcb-tools plugin write it (180 minus KiCad's bottom angle): seen from the bottom, the part is JLC's
footprint turned counter-clockwise by the rotation. U1 and J1 are at 180, U2 at 0, U3 at 180, U4 at 0, Y1 at

has a pin 1 corner mark at U1 and pin 1 dots at U2, U3, U4 and Y1 for the placement preview
([ordering.md](ordering.md#placement-preview)).

35 diodes (1N4148W): one per key, five for the RANGE switches, one each for the ALT/HDG and FMS pushes.
The RANGE control's "Push" pin is common to its five switches; centre push = com without A-D.

Each of the seven columns has a 10 k pull-up to 3V3 (R_PU0 to R_PU6, C25804), on its bus between key
rows 1 and 2. KeyMatrix drives a row low and reads the columns at once, then drives that row high and
waits about 1 us before the next row; after a row with a pressed key, a column has to come back up through
the RP2040's own pull-up (50 to 80 k) with 20 to 30 pF of copper (90 to 170 mm) and five diodes on it, a
time constant of 1 to 2.4 us, long enough for a phantom press in the same column of the next row. The
released row is driven high, so the pressed key's diode turns off at once and only the column's pull-ups
lift it: with 10 k in parallel (a time constant of 0.2 to 0.3 us) the column passes the input high
threshold well under 1 us later. A pressed key draws about 0.3 mA through its pull-ups.

The encoders feed the RP2040 directly: CircuitPython's `rotaryio` decodes the quadrature in one PIO state
machine per ring (7 of the 8), with no polling and no external decoder. A quadrature-to-up/down converter
such as the LS7183 (a Reddit suggestion) would add seven chips for a job the PIO already does. The part to
test on the Pico is the detent-to-pulse mapping and the pulse timing towards MobiFlight, not the decoding.
The encoders' metal mounting lugs are plated slots on GND (through the plane), an ESD path from the shaft.

## USB protection

U4, an SRV05-4 ESD array (hongjiacheng, C7420376, SOT-23-6), sits right behind J1: four channels of steering
diodes to its VCC pin and a clamp from VCC to GND (breakdown at least 6.0 V at 1 mA), 1.0 pF per channel to
GND, ±30 kV contact discharge (IEC 61000-4-2) and 4 A at 13.5 V typical, 15 V maximum (8/20 µs). D+ and D-
each run through two channels: from J1 into pin 6 (D+) or 4 (D-), under the body to the opposite pin 1 or 3
and on from there, so the array is in line with no stub, and each line sees 2 pF, far less than full speed
tolerates. Its pins are 4.5 mm of copper from J1's farthest contact. VCC is on VBUS_F, behind the fuse (over
the top layer to a via on the line from the fuse to the regulator), GND on a via under the body: a clamp that
fails short, or U4 placed turned by 180 degrees (its VCC on GND, the clamp diode forward across VBUS_F), then
sits behind R_IN and the fuse instead of shorting the host's VBUS (see below for what gives way). A
USBLC6-2SC6 does the same job with the same pinout, but at JLCPCB it is an extended part with a fee in both
assembly variants; the SRV05-4 is a preferred extended part with ample stock ([Ordering](ordering.md)).
`check.py` cuts U4's pads out of the bottom copper and checks that J1's contacts then no longer reach the 27 R
resistors (no copper runs past the array), and that U4 is at most 5 mm of copper from J1.

F1, a Bourns MF-NSMF050-2 PTC fuse (C75464, 1206), and R_IN, a 1 ohm 0603 resistor (C22936, basic), sit in
series in VBUS between J1 and everything else: J1, R_IN, F1, then VBUS_F with C_IN, the regulator and U4's
VCC. F1 holds 0.5 A and trips at 1.0 A (23 °C; 0.40 A hold at 50 °C), is rated 13.2 V and 100 A, and has 0.15
to 0.7 ohm. The board draws about 70 mA at most (the RP2040 with CircuitPython a few tens of mA, the flash up
to 25 mA while writing, the two LEDs up to 10 mA, 0.3 mA per pressed key in the matrix). For a short on VBUS_F
(a bridge across C_IN, a failed U3, U4 shorted or placed turned) R_IN, not F1, is what gives way: R_IN is a
0.1 W part, and at 1 A (where F1 takes about 7 s to trip) it already takes 1 W, at 1.6 A (F1: about 0.25 s)
2.6 W, at 2.5 A (about 70 ms) 6 W and at 3.5 A (about 25 ms) 12 W. A port that limits its current at 0.5 to 1
A may never trip F1 at all and leaves R_IN at 0.25 to 1 W. So R_IN overheats and opens, or drifts or scorches,
before F1 trips in practically every case: it is the sacrificial element for a short behind it, and F1 covers
slow overloads below that. Either way the host's port and the cable are protected, but after such a fault
R_IN, and perhaps F1 (and U4 if it was turned or shorted), must be replaced. A larger R_IN would not change
that (a 1206 1 ohm takes 0.25 W, still 4 to 48 times over at these currents), and does not fit between the
VBUS via and the fuse without moving F1 into R_CC2 and the crystal. F1 will not reliably trip on a fault
behind the regulator either: the XC6206's own current limit handles those, and it can let 0.35 to 0.5 A
through before it folds back, around F1's hold current when warm. Even at 100 mA (the XC6206's dropout figure)
and the lowest VBUS a USB 2.0 port may deliver, 4.40 V, R_IN and F1 (at most 1.7 ohm together) leave the
regulator 4.23 V, and its maximum dropout of 0.68 V at 100 mA still leaves 3.3 V out (typical dropout 0.25 V).
`check.py` checks that each net of the chain holds only its pads (VBUS: J1 and R_IN; VBUS_R: R_IN and F1;
VBUS_F: F1, C_IN, U3 and U4's VCC), and that with either part's pads cut out of the copper, the copper on its
input side no longer reaches anything on its output side and no copper island touches both of its pads:
nothing runs past or across them.

The XC6206 is rated 7.0 V absolute maximum, and C_IN is a ceramic capacitor with next to no loss, so VBUS
switched on hard rings with the cable's inductance. That happens when the board is plugged into a live USB-A
port (with an A to C cable); a USB-C source switches VBUS on only after it sees the 5.1k pull-downs, and with
a controlled slope. R_IN damps it: the cable's inductance, the series resistance (cable, R_IN, F1) and C_IN
form a series circuit that 1 ohm takes to critical damping or past it (2 x √(L / C) is 0.8 to 1.3 ohm up to 1
µH, and the capacitance rises again as the voltage falls), so the input settles on the supply voltage without
ringing above it on the usual cables. The model behind that: a 5.25 V step, 0.3 to 3 µH and 0.05 to 0.4 ohm of
cable, F1 at 0.15 to 0.7 ohm, no load and no contact bounce, C_IN with the DC-bias loss of a 10 V X5R 0603 (10
uF nominal, about 3 uF at 5 V and 2.3 to 2.5 uF at 7 V, and a pessimistic curve with 1.8 uF at 5 V and 1.4 uF
at 7 V), and U4's clamp left out, the worst case (the datasheet gives only a minimum breakdown of 6.0 V at 1
mA, and about 6 V at 0.5 A to 13 V at 4 A in its figure 4). Up to 1 µH every case stays within 0.05 V of 5.25
V; long cables (2 to 3 µH) with little resistance ring up to about 6 V (5.8 V on the nominal curves, 6.6 V on
the pessimistic one), under 7.0 V in every case modelled. Without R_IN the same model rang up to 9.7 V on a
short, low-resistance cable with no clamp, 2.7 V over the regulator's maximum and above it for about 3 µs;
0.33 ohm would have brought the worst case to 6.4 V and 0.5 ohm to 5.7 V, but 1 ohm is the lowest basic value.
R_IN takes the inrush: at most 4.1 A, about 17 W at its peak and above half of that current for 5 to 7 µs,
under 0.1 mJ in all. The measure for that is pulse power: an 0603 thick-film resistor takes about 25 W for a 5
µs single pulse (Vishay's CRCW0603 pulse curve, as a stand-in for the Uniroyal part), about 1.5 times the
peak. A separate TVS diode would not have done it: an SMF5.0A breaks down at 6.4 to 7.0 V and clamps no lower.

With 10 uF on VBUS_F the board is at the 10 uF the USB 2.0 specification (7.2.4.1) allows a device to show on
VBUS at attach, and the capacitance behind the regulator (C_OUT0's 10 uF, C_D2 and C_BULK, the 100 nF
capacitors) adds to what the host sees while VBUS comes up; R_IN and the fuse limit that inrush. So the board
is at the limit and, counting those, over its intent; on PC ports that is no practical issue.

## Lights

The board's two lights are XINGLIGHT XL-302UYC (yellow 588 nm, the
closest 3 mm amber, C2895466) with 330 R (C23138), and XL-302UWC (white, C2895458) with 22 R (C23345).
Amber runs at about 3 mA from the GPIO. The white LED's forward voltage is 2.7 to 3.0 V at a few mA, close
to the 3.3 V supply, so it gets a small resistor: roughly 2 to 7 mA over that spread. The firmware sets both
brightnesses with PWM. Check the white light on the first board; if it is too dim, raise `LED_DUTY[1]` in
`code.py` first, then bridge R_UNIT's pads or fit a 0 R (C21189) ([Firmware](firmware.md)).
The LEDs sit where their light holes keep at least 0.8 mm of aluminium to the next key hole (JLCCNC's
minimum wall): 0.85 mm for SHIFT, 0.86 mm for UNIT (0.75 and 0.76 mm with both holes at their +0.1 limit,
above JLCCNC's 0.5 mm absolute minimum). Each series resistor lies on the bottom in line beside its LED's
anode pad, the anode end 0.55 mm from it (the same net, so a bridge or a remelt is harmless) and the
GPIO end 2.15 mm away, so the hand-soldered LED pins have no pad of another net within 1.0 mm (beside
rather than below: the SHIFT light is 5 mm from the board edge, and every part body but J1's keeps the
2.5 mm JLCPCB's assembly terms ask for).
